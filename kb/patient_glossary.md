# Patient glossary
source: plain-language explanations written for this project
audience: patient

## Nodule
A lung nodule is a small spot in the lung that shows up on a scan. Most small nodules are not cancer. They are often old scars or healed infections. Doctors watch them over time to see whether they change.

## Solid nodule
A solid nodule looks dense on the scan, so the normal lung behind it cannot be seen through it.

## Ground-glass nodule
A ground-glass nodule looks hazy, like frosted glass. The lung structures behind it can still be seen. These usually change very slowly and are followed with repeat scans.

## Part-solid nodule
A part-solid nodule has a hazy part and a dense part. Doctors pay most attention to the size of the dense part.

## Mean diameter
The size of a nodule is given in millimetres. The mean diameter is the average of its longest and shortest width on the scan. For scale, 6 millimetres is about the width of a pencil eraser.

## Lung-RADS category
Lung-RADS is a scale radiologists use to describe a lung screening scan. Category 1 and 2 mean nothing worrying was found. Category 3 means a finding is probably harmless but should be checked again sooner than usual. Category 4 means a finding needs a closer look, which often means another scan and sometimes more tests. A category is not a diagnosis.

## Follow-up scan
A follow-up scan is a repeat CT after a set time, often 3, 6 or 12 months. Comparing two scans shows whether a nodule has stayed the same, shrunk or grown. Staying the same over time is reassuring.

## Low-dose CT
A low-dose CT is a chest scan that uses less radiation than a standard CT. It is the usual scan for lung screening and for following nodules.

## PET/CT
A PET/CT is a scan that shows how active tissue is. It can help doctors decide whether a nodule needs a sample taken.

## Biopsy or tissue sampling
A biopsy means taking a very small sample of tissue so it can be examined under a microscope. It is the way to know for certain what a nodule is.

## Calcification
Calcification is a build-up of calcium in a nodule. Certain patterns of calcification are a sign that a nodule is harmless.

## When to contact your care team urgently
Contact your care team or emergency services straight away if you cough up blood, have chest pain, have new or worsening shortness of breath, or feel very unwell. This assistant cannot assess symptoms.
