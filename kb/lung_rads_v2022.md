# Lung-RADS v2022 summary
source: American College of Radiology, Lung-RADS v2022 assessment categories (summary written for this project; consult the ACR document for clinical use)
audience: clinician

## Measurement
Nodules are measured on lung windows. The mean diameter is the average of the long axis and the short axis taken on the same image, and it is reported to one decimal place in millimetres. Growth means an increase in mean diameter of more than 1.5 mm within a 12-month interval. Slice thickness for nodule measurement should be 2.5 mm or less.

## Category 1 negative
No lung nodules, or nodules with a benign calcification pattern: complete, central, popcorn or concentric-ring calcification, or nodules that contain fat. Management: continue annual screening with low-dose CT in 12 months.

## Category 2 benign appearance or behaviour
Solid nodule smaller than 6 mm at baseline, or a new solid nodule smaller than 4 mm. Part-solid nodule smaller than 6 mm in total diameter at baseline. Non-solid (ground-glass) nodule smaller than 30 mm at baseline, new or growing, or 30 mm or larger and stable or slowly growing. Management: continue annual screening with low-dose CT in 12 months.

## Category 3 probably benign
Solid nodule 6 mm to less than 8 mm at baseline, or a new solid nodule 4 mm to less than 6 mm. Part-solid nodule 6 mm or larger in total diameter with a solid component smaller than 6 mm at baseline, or a new part-solid nodule smaller than 6 mm in total diameter. Non-solid nodule 30 mm or larger at baseline or new. Management: low-dose CT in 6 months.

## Category 4A suspicious
Solid nodule 8 mm to less than 15 mm at baseline, a growing solid nodule smaller than 8 mm, or a new solid nodule 6 mm to less than 8 mm. Part-solid nodule 6 mm or larger with a solid component 6 mm to less than 8 mm at baseline, or with a new or growing solid component smaller than 4 mm. Management: low-dose CT in 3 months; PET/CT may be considered when there is a solid nodule or solid component of 8 mm or larger.

## Category 4B very suspicious
Solid nodule 15 mm or larger at baseline, or a new or growing solid nodule 8 mm or larger. Part-solid nodule with a solid component 8 mm or larger at baseline, or with a new or growing solid component 4 mm or larger. Management: diagnostic chest CT with or without contrast; PET/CT may be considered when there is a solid nodule or solid component of 8 mm or larger; tissue sampling or referral for further clinical evaluation, depending on the probability of malignancy and comorbidities.

## Category 4X additional suspicious features
A category 3 or 4 nodule with additional features or imaging findings that increase the suspicion of lung cancer, such as spiculation, lymphadenopathy or frank metastatic disease. Management is as for category 4B.

## Exam category
The overall exam category is that of the most suspicious nodule. An exam with no nodules is category 1.

## Scope
Lung-RADS applies to lung cancer screening examinations in eligible high-risk individuals. It is not designed for incidental nodules found on CT performed for other reasons; the Fleischner Society recommendations cover those.
