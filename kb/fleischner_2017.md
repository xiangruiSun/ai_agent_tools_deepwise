# Fleischner Society 2017 incidental nodule summary
source: MacMahon H et al., Guidelines for Management of Incidental Pulmonary Nodules Detected on CT Images: From the Fleischner Society 2017, Radiology 2017 (summary written for this project; consult the paper for clinical use)
audience: clinician

## Scope
These recommendations apply to incidental nodules in adults aged 35 years or older. They do not apply to lung cancer screening, to patients with known primary cancer, or to immunosuppressed patients.

## Single solid nodule smaller than 6 mm
Low-risk patient: no routine follow-up. High-risk patient: optional CT at 12 months.

## Single solid nodule 6 to 8 mm
Low-risk patient: CT at 6 to 12 months, then consider CT at 18 to 24 months. High-risk patient: CT at 6 to 12 months, then CT at 18 to 24 months.

## Single solid nodule larger than 8 mm
For both low-risk and high-risk patients: consider CT at 3 months, PET/CT, or tissue sampling.

## Multiple solid nodules
Smaller than 6 mm: no routine follow-up in low-risk patients; optional CT at 12 months in high-risk patients. 6 mm or larger: CT at 3 to 6 months, then consider CT at 18 to 24 months; in high-risk patients the 18 to 24 month CT is recommended. Management is based on the most suspicious nodule.

## Single ground-glass nodule
Smaller than 6 mm: no routine follow-up. 6 mm or larger: CT at 6 to 12 months to confirm persistence, then CT every 2 years until 5 years.

## Single part-solid nodule
Smaller than 6 mm: no routine follow-up. 6 mm or larger: CT at 3 to 6 months to confirm persistence; if unchanged and the solid component remains smaller than 6 mm, annual CT for 5 years. A persistent part-solid nodule with a solid component of 6 mm or larger is highly suspicious.

## Risk factors
High risk is suggested by older age, heavy smoking, upper-lobe location, spiculated margins, emphysema or fibrosis, and a family history of lung cancer.
